NARRATION PERFORMANCE RULES (for horror stories)

1. No timestamps or headings in the spoken script. Remove labels like [00:00–00:45], "The Hook," and "Final Reveal."

2. Don't read sound effects aloud. Remove [SFX: footsteps], [MUSIC: piano], and similar editing instructions from the narration text. Add those separately during editing.

3. Use natural punctuation. Commas, periods, and occasional ellipses help guide delivery. Don't add a dramatic pause after every sentence.

4. Avoid overacting. Start like you're telling a friend something strange that happened to you. Let fear develop gradually.

5. Change your emotional delivery. Begin tired and slightly sarcastic, become confused and uneasy, then vulnerable and emotional. The ending should be quiet and sincere.

6. Keep dialogue believable. The old woman should sound calm and ordinary, not like a stereotypical evil character.

7. Don't force a whisper. Use a lower, softer voice only when the character genuinely feels frightened or emotionally overwhelmed.

8. Leave room for silence. Add pauses before important revelations, not after every scary sentence.

9. Avoid robotic pacing. Vary sentence lengths and let some lines flow together naturally.

10. Keep the narration separate from editing directions. Your voice generator or narrator should receive only the words meant to be spoken.
